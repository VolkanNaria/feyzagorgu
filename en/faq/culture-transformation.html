About Culture Transformation

The questions CEOs, HR directors and boards of directors are most curious about, and the answers.

Home → FAQ → Culture Transformation

The questions below are the ones I am asked most often by senior executives and HR leaders who are considering a transformation journey in their organizations. I have answered each one drawing on my own experience and more than 25 years of corporate practice.

Culture transformation is the process of aligning an organization's values, leadership approach, ways of working and employee experience with its strategic goals. The aim is not only to change behaviors, but to create an organization that supports sustainable business results.

Company culture directly affects:

  • Decision-making speed
  • Collaboration
  • Innovation
  • Employee engagement
  • Customer experience
  • Leadership quality

Strong cultures make it easier to put strategy into practice.

Culture transformation is not a training project but a change journey.

  • First gains: 3–6 months
  • Behavior change: 6–12 months
  • Lasting cultural transformation: 18–36 months

The duration depends on the company's size, leadership support and current maturity level.

The foundations of employee engagement are trust, meaning, development opportunities and good leadership. The first step is to understand the current situation. Strengths and areas for improvement are identified through employee experience analyses, engagement surveys and focus group sessions. Afterwards, prioritized actions are implemented in the areas of leadership quality, communication, career development and work experience.

Middle managers are the critical point where organizational culture is carried into daily life. This is why, beyond technical expertise, they need to be supported with the following skills:

  • Communication
  • Giving feedback
  • Coaching
  • Performance management
  • Conflict management
  • Change leadership

Strong middle managers act as a bridge between strategy and employee experience.

Culture is not preserved automatically as a natural result of growth. The following elements need to be aligned with the same cultural framework:

  • Company values
  • Leadership principles
  • Hiring processes
  • Performance systems
  • Development programs

The aim in growth is not to preserve the past, but to develop the culture consciously without losing the company's essence.

In the age of AI, leaders are expected to have not only technical knowledge but also human competencies. The skills that stand out:

  • Learning agility

The most effective way to manage different generations is not to label people by age group, but to build a shared purpose, inclusive leadership and flexible working approaches. While different expectations are natural, learning opportunities, meaningful work, development and the need to be appreciated are of common importance to all generations.

Culture can be measured through perceptions and behaviors. To do this, the following are assessed together:

  • Employee engagement surveys
  • Culture surveys
  • Leadership assessments
  • Focus group sessions
  • Workforce turnover rate
  • Internal promotion rates
  • Customer satisfaction indicators
  • Collaboration and agility indicators

Culture that cannot be measured cannot be managed.

Resistance is often not a reaction to change itself, but a natural response to uncertainty and loss of control. This is why the following are important in change processes:

  • Transparent communication
  • Employee involvement in the process
  • Managers acting as role models
  • Making early success stories visible

The aim is not to eliminate resistance, but to channel its energy into the transformation.

There is no single magic formula for retaining talent. However, research and practice show that the following factors are decisive:

  • Strong and trustworthy leadership
  • Fair and transparent career opportunities
  • Learning and development opportunities
  • Flexibility and employee experience
  • Meaningful work and a sense of purpose
  • A culture of appreciation and feedback
  • A climate of psychological safety
  • Competitive pay and benefits

Employees often leave not companies but their experiences. This is why retaining talent requires a holistic employee experience design, not just a pay policy.

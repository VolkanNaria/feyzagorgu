What Is an Assessment & Development Center?

Performance or potential? 7 indicators of high potential

← All Articles

Assessment centers are an evaluation tool designed to measure not a person's current performance, but their potential to progress into larger and more complex leadership roles in the future. They focus less on the question "Is this person successful today?" and more on "Can this person take on more senior leadership roles in the future?" In other words, the answer to the question of what potential is lies in a person's ability to take on future roles.

Performance ≠ Potential

Not everyone who performs highly has high potential. For example, a high performer at a bank branch who processes transactions very quickly and therefore outpaces colleagues in volume may not have the same level of potential. The reverse also holds: an employee with high potential who is doing a job far below their capabilities may show low performance.

For this reason, relying solely on performance evaluation in talent management processes such as promotion and identifying development needs is risky.

The 7 Key Indicators of High Potential

1. Drive to Advance

Does this person want the next position up? The answer to "Who wouldn't?" is not always yes: if a good salesperson is happy with their current position and the bonuses they earn, it is perfectly understandable that they would not want to lead a sales team. Or a CFO who dreams of travelling the world and wants to look after their grandchildren may not want the CEO role.

2. Experience

Has this person had the necessary critical experiences? The key word here is "critical". For example, a candidate for finance manager should have basic accounting knowledge. Team management experience is a plus, but if they have not managed a team and they have learning agility, they can still be a candidate for that position.

3. Awareness

How well does this person know themselves and their impact? This means being aware of both their strengths and development areas, as well as their goals and passions; of how they affect their teammates and the organization; and of their organization's priorities.

4. Learning Agility

Can they learn from experience and adapt to new situations? Not everyone needs to know every new program or situation in advance — the real question is whether they can solve new situations with the knowledge they have and transfer that learning.

5. Leadership Competencies

Do they have the competencies critical for leadership? These are generally competencies that companies define internally: can they communicate effectively, what is their decision-making ability, can they take initiative, can they demonstrate effective coaching/mentoring?

6. Capacity

What is meant here is cognitive capacity — can they analyze and solve complex problems?

7. Derailment Risk

At what point do their strengths become a risk? For example, does micromanagement trip up a finance director who was promoted thanks to their attention to detail and knowledge of regulations? Or, if the person has been promoted from a more technical role into a position that requires more flexibility, does being "closed" to new ideas create various difficulties?

Assessment center practices, within talent management, seek answers to the 7 questions above and use various realistic tools to find those answers. Role plays, strategic planning exercises, in-tray (agenda) exercises, competency-based interviews (generally using the STAR technique), general ability tests and personality inventories are the most commonly used tools.

Conclusion

Because promotion, development planning and readiness for the next position are determined through assessment centers based on the assessed person's performance and potential, this practice is both highly beneficial and critical.

It will be very helpful for people who will go through this process to get support from a coach who is an expert in the subject. During these coaching sessions, the person will discover their own awareness while also learning about assessment center tools and getting the chance to practise them first-hand, and will therefore get the maximum benefit from the process. The input they receive from an objective person will also allow them to develop themselves much faster.

At the organizational level, such assessment systems are critically important especially for companies in the growth and institutionalization process — for example, as part of talent management within TURQUALITY® preparation processes.
